    Feature 01

    Claim DNA

    Break a claim into the details that shape its meaning—not just the overall similarity.

    Compare two versions of a claim to review changes in meaning, numbers, negation, and certainty.
    WHO / WHOSE

    People, groups, or populations described.

    WHAT / ACTION

    Main event, action, or relationship.

    WHEN / WHERE

    Time, place, and setting.

    NUMBERS

    Amounts, percentages, and quantities.

    NEGATION

    Words such as not, never, or no.

    CERTAINTY

    Words such as may, suggests, or proves.

    This preview explains the elements that can change when a claim is repeated. Automatic Claim DNA extraction is not connected yet.
    Feature 02

    Missing Context Detector

    Identify important qualifiers, limitations, and conditions that may disappear when a claim is repeated.

    Compare the original passage with the later version. Review whether uncertainty, study limitations, and conditions are preserved.
    Certainty Words

    May, might, suggests, preliminary.

    Scope Limitations

    Some people, a small study, this sample.

    Conditions

    Only when, under specific conditions, in a trial.

    What to review

    Check whether the later version removes uncertainty, narrows or broadens the population, or omits important study limitations.

    This interface is a preview. Automatic context detection has not been connected yet.

    About AI TruthTrail

    Information deserves its full context.

    AI TruthTrail is a prototype for exploring how information changes as it is repeated. It focuses on wording, context, certainty, and the path a claim takes between versions—not on presenting an unexplained true-or-false label.

    Follow the changes. Wording · Context · Certainty · Evidence
    Why it matters

    A claim can change while it travels.

    One small word can turn a cautious statement into a confident one. TruthTrail is designed to make those differences easier to inspect.

    01 · ORIGINAL

    Keep the first version

    Start with the source wording, including limitations and uncertainty.

    02 · COMPARE

    Spot the shift

    Review changes in wording, certainty, scope, and possible context loss.

    03 · REVIEW

    Check the evidence

    Use the results as a guide, then inspect reliable sources before drawing conclusions.
